Is Tap Water Safe to Drink in Saint Lucia?

Tap water in Saint Lucia is treated by the Water and Sewerage Company (WASCO), sourced mainly from rainforest rivers, but caution is advised because heavy rain frequently causes turbidity spikes and localized boil-water advisories.

Water Source

WASCO draws primarily from rivers in Saint Lucia's mountainous, rainforest interior, including the Roseau and Troumassee watersheds, which are treated at plants before distribution to Castries, Soufrière, and other towns.

Contaminants & Concerns

Heavy tropical rainfall, common on this volcanic island, frequently increases turbidity and organic matter in river sources faster than treatment plants can fully process, leading to periodic boil-water advisories, particularly after storms.

Regional Variations

Castries and the northern tourist corridor generally have more consistent supply, but bottled water remains the safer choice for visitors. Soufrière and rural southern areas near the Pitons are more prone to turbidity-related advisories after rain.

Water Hardness

Saint Lucia's river-sourced water is generally soft, roughly 20–70 mg/L..

Do Locals Drink Tap Water?

Many Saint Lucians drink tap water under normal conditions but pay close attention to boil-water notices after storms.

Tips for Travelers

  • •Check for active boil-water advisories with WASCO, especially after heavy rain
  • •Resorts near the Pitons and Soufrière often provide filtered water — ask staff to confirm
  • •Carry a portable filter if hiking or staying in rainforest interior lodges

Recommended Precautions

  • •Avoid tap water during and after heavy rain or storms until advisories are lifted
  • •Confirm water treatment status at rainforest lodges and rural guesthouses
