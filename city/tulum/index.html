Is Tap Water Safe to Drink in Tulúm?

No, tap water in Tulúm is not safe to drink. The rapidly growing town is served by Aguakan and a patchwork of private wells tapping into the Yucatán Peninsula’s porous limestone aquifer, which is highly vulnerable to contamination.

Water Source

Tulúm’s water comes from deep wells drilled into the Yucatán karst aquifer, the same cenote-connected underground river system that stretches across the peninsula. Aguakan provides some municipal distribution, but many hotels and developments operate independent well systems.

Contaminants & Concerns

The porous karst geology allows untreated wastewater, septic leachate, and agricultural chemicals to percolate rapidly into the aquifer. Tulúm’s explosive tourism growth has outpaced sewage treatment capacity, and studies have found elevated faecal coliform, nitrate, and pharmaceutical residues in cenote-connected groundwater.

Tips

  • •Drink only purified bottled water — the cenote aquifer is vulnerable to contamination regardless of depth
  • •Use purified water for brushing teeth and rinsing produce, even in eco-hotels and beach clubs
  • •When visiting cenotes for swimming, avoid swallowing water — the same aquifer feeds both cenotes and taps
