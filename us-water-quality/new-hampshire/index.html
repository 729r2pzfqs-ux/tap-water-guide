Overview

New Hampshire's public systems, Manchester's Lake Massabesic supply chief among them, are clean and compliant. But nearly half the state drinks from private bedrock wells, and that is where New Hampshire's two signature contaminants live: arsenic and PFAS.

The state's granite bedrock naturally yields arsenic (New Hampshire adopted a 5 ppb standard, twice as strict as EPA's), and the Saint-Gobain plastics plant in Merrimack created one of New England's major PFAS plumes, extending bottled water and treatment to thousands of properties. New Hampshire was among the first states to set enforceable PFAS limits.

Common Contaminants in New Hampshire

Arsenic in bedrock wells

Roughly one in five private wells exceeds the state's strict 5 ppb standard; testing rates remain the public-health gap

PFAS

The Merrimack Saint-Gobain plume plus scattered military/industrial sites; state limits among the nation's first, treatment ongoing

Radon and uranium

Granite geology yields radionuclides in some wells; mitigation is well established

Violations & Compliance

Public systems show strong compliance, with PFAS remediation the active front. The state's focus is private wells, which serve almost half of residents outside any federal rule.

Water Hardness in New Hampshire

Soft, typically 10–50 mg/L. Granite-bedrock reservoirs and lakes keep New Hampshire's municipal water consistently soft.
